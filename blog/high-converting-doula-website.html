Doula Website Strategy

5 Mistakes I See on Doula Websites That Quietly Cost Inquiries

A doula website can feel warm, thoughtful, and aligned with your work — and still make it harder for the right people to reach out. These are the patterns I see most often when a site is not converting the way it should.

Website strategy • Doula websites • User flow

Most doula websites are not “bad.” In fact, many of them feel caring, intentional, and aligned with the work behind them.

But that is not always what determines whether someone reaches out.

When a website is not bringing in consistent inquiries, it is usually not because people dislike what you offer. It is because the site is making them pause, sort, or second-guess what to do next.

That kind of friction is often subtle. But it adds up quickly.

1. The homepage sounds warm, but says very little

A lot of doula websites lean heavily on language that feels kind and supportive. That matters - but on its own, it is not enough.

If someone lands on your site and cannot quickly understand who you help, what kind of support you offer, and what they should do next, they start filling in the gaps themselves.

And most people will not do that for long.

A strong homepage does not need more words. It needs clearer ones.

2. Too many offers are competing for attention

Birth support, postpartum support, classes, digital products, memberships - none of these are the problem on their own.

The problem is when everything is presented at the same level, without structure.

When a website feels like a list instead of a guided experience, people have to stop and sort through what applies to them. That hesitation often leads to them leaving instead of choosing.

A better-performing site gives each offer a clear place and a clear role within the overall flow.

3. The next step is not obvious enough

One of the biggest issues I see is not a lack of calls to action - it is a lack of clear direction.

Buttons are there, but they are easy to miss. Or they feel disconnected from what someone just read. Or the next step itself is unclear.

People should not have to hunt for how to reach out or wonder what happens after they do.

A strong website makes the next step feel calm, visible, and expected.

4. The mobile experience feels like an afterthought

Most visitors are seeing your website on their phone first.

If sections feel oversized, cards stack awkwardly, text is hard to read, or spacing feels off, trust drops quickly - even if the desktop version looks good.

A high-converting site is not just responsive. It is intentionally designed for how people actually move through it on mobile.

5. The website feels pieced together instead of guided

This is the one that is hardest to spot, but it makes a big difference.

When pages do not connect well, when the shop feels separate from the services, or when the flow between sections is unclear, the experience starts to feel disjointed.

Even if each individual piece is well-designed, the overall site can still feel harder to trust.

A strong website feels like one continuous experience - not several parts stitched together.

What this means for your website

If your site is getting traffic but not many inquiries, the issue is rarely your business.

More often, it is the structure, the messaging, or the flow between pages that needs to be refined.

This is exactly what I broke down in this case study, where a few structural changes led to four discovery calls within 48 hours.

The takeaway

A high-performing doula website does not rely on pressure or over-selling.

It works because it reduces friction. It helps people feel oriented, understood, and confident in what to do next.

When that happens, everything else gets easier - trust builds faster, decisions feel simpler, and the right people are more likely to reach out.

If your website feels like it should be working better than it is, it may not need a full rebuild.

It may just need clearer structure, stronger messaging, and a more intentional flow.
